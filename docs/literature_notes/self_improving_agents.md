## Q1. Does any paper compare acceptance/selection rules (not proposers) of multiple self-improving/evolving agents in a shared environment, especially with Kantian/universalization or welfare-based acceptance?

### Takeaway
No paper found does this. The literature splits into (a) single-agent self-improvement papers that have begun to treat the acceptance gate as the object of study (SAGE, PACE, Skill-Policy trap), but all in single-agent settings, with SAGE and PACE explicitly naming multi-agent acceptance as open future work; (b) multi-agent social-dilemma work that varies agents' objectives or reasoning (Kantian prompts, universalization prompting in GovSim, SVO/prosocial reward shaping, difference/local/global fitness in cooperative coevolution) but not as an accept/reject gate on proposed self-modifications at matched search budget; and (c) group-level rule evolution (evolved constitutions) whose fitness is collective but which is centralized search, not per-agent gates. The closest classical analogue is the Agogino–Tumer "local vs. global vs. difference evaluation" comparison in multiagent coevolution, which is a fitness-assignment comparison in fully cooperative settings, not a mixed-motive acceptance-gate comparison.

### Cited Findings
**Acceptance gate as experimental object, single agent only**
- SAGE (arXiv:2609.36043, 28 Sep 2026) proposes a paired, one-sided exact binomial acceptance gate with a regression cap and abstention; compared against the SkillOpt "keep if aggregate validation score strictly rises" gate, with equal proposed edits and equal token cost (Sec. 4.1). Experiments are single-agent only. Sec. 5.3 "Future Work": "Multi-agent acceptance is a natural and harder extension of the view taken here," because "the gain or regression of one edit is no longer decidable from local evidence alone." Uses a "first-do-no-harm principle" to weight regressions (Sec. 3.2), but no welfare/Kantian terms. Read in full. — [SAGE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36043v1)
- PACE (arXiv:2606.08106, 6 Jun 2026, single independent author) compares greedy, paired fixed-n (McNemar-style), online-FDR, and an anytime-valid e-process commit gate for a single prompt-evolving Qwen2.5 agent; budget is not strictly matched (PACE uses ~18% fewer evaluations than greedy). Multi-agent topologies appear only as an extension "argued, not shown" (Sec. 1, 6). Read in full. — [PACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08106v1)
- "Evolving Together, Failing Apart" (arXiv:2610.20845, 14 Oct 2026) formalizes an "idealized cooperative acceptance" rule (skill bank chosen to maximize current policy return) and catalogs gates of existing methods (success-gated, Thompson sampling, marginal utility, reward thresholds, contract verification; Table 1) but co-evolution is between one agent's policy and its own skill bank; no head-to-head gate comparison, no multiple payoff-bearing agents. Read in full (first ~100k chars). — [Skill–Policy trap](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20845v1)

**Kantian/universalization in multi-agent LLM settings, but not as an acceptance gate over self-modification**
- GovSim (Piatti et al., arXiv:2404.16698) is a common-pool resource simulation for LLM agents; agents using universalization-based reasoning achieved significantly greater sustainability, and most LLMs fail to reach a sustainable equilibrium. This is prompting at action time, not gating of self-improvements. Abstract/snippet only. — [GovSim](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16698v2)
- "Kantian Equilibrium in the Age of Multi-Agent Systems" (I. Samoylenko, NExT-Game workshop @ ICML 2026, OpenReview id hizxbjrwxO): one-shot common-pool game (R=20) with k=0..5 of 5 LLM agents prompted as Kantian ("what if everyone did the same") vs. Nashian; more Kantian agents raised total welfare, moving toward the social optimum of 4 units from the Nash mean of 6.4. No learning, no evolution, no acceptance rule; training and repeated play listed as future work. Read via aggregator summary only (not the OpenReview PDF). — [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/kantian-equilibrium-in-the-age-of-multi-agent-systems/wrk_1d667867ebbfe444c48d2acd64b014a9)
- Economics: Alger & Weibull show preferences mixing self-interest with Kantian morality ("Homo moralis", degree κ) are evolutionarily stable under assortative matching (Econometrica 2013; GEB 2016; 2023 correction to instability part). This is the theoretical origin of the κ-mixture but concerns preference evolution, not algorithmic acceptance gates. Snippet only. — [TSE: Evolution and Kantian morality](https://www.tse-fr.eu/articles/evolution-and-kantian-morality?lang=en)
- Roemer-style Kantian optimization: 2026 note argues the reference point matters and that with the Nash action as reference, the Kantian type weakly dominates and is evolutionarily stable; vulnerability to free-riding by Nash optimizers is noted. Snippet only. — [arXiv:2605.00692](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00692); see also partial Kantian cooperation [arXiv:1609.01921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.01921)

**Group-level rule evolution with collective fitness (centralized search, not per-agent gates)**
- "Evolving Interpretable Constitutions for Multi-Agent Coordination" (arXiv:2602.00755, 31 Jan 2026): OpenEvolve multi-island search over a society-wide constitution, fitness = Stability Score S = max(0, 0.5P + 0.3V − 0.2C), weights motivated by utilitarian/Rawlsian welfare theory (Sec. 2.4), conflict treated as a negative externality. Compares constitutions and single vs. multi-island, not selection criteria or a tax. Read in full. — [arXiv:2602.00755](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00755)

**Cooperative coevolution fitness assignment (closest classical analogue)**
- Agogino & Tumer, "Efficient Evaluation Functions for Evolving Coordination" (Evolutionary Computation 16(2), 2008): compares local, global, and difference (marginal-contribution) evaluation functions for evolving per-agent policies in a multi-rover domain; aligned + agent-sensitive (difference) evaluations outperform global by up to 400% (600% in GECCO 2004 version). Colby & Tumer (AAMAS 2012) apply difference evaluations to cooperative coevolution (+up to 50%); a 2015 replicator-dynamics analysis gives conditions under which difference evaluations favor optimal actions. Snippet level only. — [GECCO 2005 Tumer & Agogino](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gecco2005/docs/p591.pdf); [AAMAS 2012](https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2012/papers/2C_2.pdf); [AAMAS 2015](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2015/aamas/p1661.pdf)

### Inferences
- The project's "not found" claim survives this search for its specific combination: multiple self-improving agents + shared environment with externalities + acceptance gate as the factor + Kantian/κ-mixture/welfare/no-harm gates + Pigouvian price comparator + matched budget. SAGE (Sep 2026) and PACE (Jun 2026) are the most useful citations because they explicitly flag multi-agent acceptance as open, which directly positions the project.
- The novelty claim should be scoped and should cite precursors: (i) Agogino–Tumer local/global/difference fitness is conceptually "private vs. joint welfare vs. unilateral (marginal) welfare" evaluation in evolving multiagent systems, albeit fully cooperative (aligned global objective) and as fitness shaping, not accept/reject of proposals; (ii) Alger–Weibull Homo moralis supplies the κ-degree Kantian mixture; (iii) GovSim and the Kantian Equilibrium workshop paper show universalization reasoning helps LLM agents in commons, but at action time; (iv) prosocial/SVO reward shaping in MARL varies objectives, not gates.
- An unverified but likely-relevant classical line not fetched: Panait/Wiegand/Luke on biasing cooperative coevolution credit assignment (optimistic vs. mean collaborator evaluation). Listed under gaps.

### Gaps
- Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar, OpenReview, GECCO and ALIFE proceedings were not queried through their own search interfaces; coverage relies on general web search. A paper under a non-obvious name (e.g., "admission rule", "commit rule", "promotion criterion" in multi-agent evolution) could have been missed.
- The Kantian Equilibrium workshop paper was read only via a third-party summary; the OpenReview PDF (id hizxbjrwxO) was not opened.
- Agogino–Tumer 2008 full text not read (author page 404); results are from snippet-level search results and the GECCO/AAMAS PDF links as indexed.
- Panait, Wiegand & Luke cooperative coevolution credit-assignment papers not verified in this session.
- "Emergent Collusion in Long-Horizon LLM Agent Interaction" (alphaxiv 2609.24967) involves ACCEPT/REJECT verdicts between agents on each other's work; seen only as a search snippet, not read.

## Q2. Full-text check of the named preprints: what each does and whether it overlaps

### Takeaway
All nine named works exist and were read in the body (first ~100k characters each). None compares acceptance rules across multiple payoff-bearing agents sharing an environment, none uses Kantian/universalization/welfare/externality gates, and none uses a price on externalities. The few with acceptance-rule variation (DGM, Hyperagents: archive vs. single-successor) vary it for a single lineage on a single objective. Overlap with the project is nil on the core factor; they are context for "propose-evaluate-accept loops".

### Cited Findings
- **CORAL** (arXiv:2604.01658; Qu et al., v1 2 Apr 2026, v3 2 Sep 2026). Long-running LLM agents with shared persistent memory, isolated git worktrees, one shared hidden evaluator; no formal acceptance rule, scores labeled "improved if strictly better, baseline if equal, regressed if worse" (App. C.2); agents choose parents themselves (Sec. 4.2). Agents pursue a common objective; interaction is informational only ("one agent's discoveries can influence another agent's future search", Sec. 3.3); no per-agent payoffs or externalities. Compared vs. OpenEvolve/ShinkaEvolve/EvoX with budgets matched by wall-clock/iterations; ablations on knowledge accumulation and co-evolution vs. independent runs (Table 3). No Kantian/welfare/externality/free-riding terms. Read in full (first 100k of 119k chars). Overlap: none on gates. — [CORAL](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01658)
- **Hyperagents / DGM-H** (arXiv:2603.19461; Zhang, Zhao, Yang, Foerster, Clune, Jiang, Devlin, Shavrina; v1 19 Mar 2026, v2 1 Nov 2026). Self-referential agents (task agent + self-modifiable meta agent) extending the DGM; every evaluated variant is added to the archive; parent selection proportional to performance and inversely to number of compiled children; selection is handcrafted and not self-modifiable in main experiments. Single archive per run; no shared environment or inter-agent externalities. Baseline "DGM-H w/o open-ended exploration" (single successor) is effectively an alternative acceptance rule at matched 100 iterations (Sec. 5.1); App. E.5 has preliminary results on modifying parent selection (not seen in the fetched portion). No Kantian/welfare terms. Latest-version HTML returned 404; v1 HTML read (first 100k of 179k). — [Hyperagents abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19461); [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19461v1)
- **MASS** (arXiv:2610.12176; Lee, Xu, Seely, Lee, Sojoudi, Zaharia, Tang; 8 Oct 2026). Alternates evolutionary search over multi-agent workflows with SFT on own trajectories. Acceptance: self-evaluator pairwise preference vs. incumbent, "an evolution process with an elite size one" (Sec. 3; App. A.1 Alg. 2). Single team objective; orchestrator and subagents are copies of one model; no externalities. Varies evaluator (self vs. an external model), optimizer and history (Sec. 5.1, App. E), but not acceptance rules at matched budget. No Kantian/welfare terms. Read in full (first 100k of 189k). — [MASS](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12176)
- **AIDE²** (arXiv:2609.26457; Srikanth, Zhao, Xu, Wu, Jiang; 22 Sep 2026). Outer-loop agent rewrites inner research agents; a rewrite is kept only if its mean held-out grade beats the incumbent (Eq. 2, 4); no margin or statistical test, and the Discussion concedes noisy comparisons can accept false improvements. Single lineage; no shared environment or externalities; acceptance rule not varied; eval budgets matched per task. Mentions Red Queen Gödel Machine (Iacob et al., 2026) only as related work. Read in full (first 100k of 122k). — [AIDE²](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26457)
- **Misevolve** (arXiv:2509.26354; Shao et al.; ICLR 2026). Measures "misevolution" across model, memory, tool, workflow pathways, each evolving a single agent/model. Mitigations: proposed but untested static-analysis + LLM-judge tool gate ("not tested in our work"); tested prompt-based refusal before external-tool ingestion (refusal rate rose from about 3–7 % to about 69 % for two tested models); post-training safety phase (59.5%→62.75% safe rate). No comparison of alternative acceptance gates; no multi-agent externalities; cites Hammond et al. on multi-agent risks only in Related Work. Read in full (first 100k of 194k). — [Misevolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26354)
- **Co-evolution survey** (arXiv:2608.10299; Zong et al.; 10 Aug 2026; taxonomy Agent–Agent / Agent–Environment / Meta co-evolution). Body: free-riding appears once, describing MAC-SPGG's sequential public-goods reward "to discourage free-riding" (Sec. 3.2.1); Sec. 4.3.1 mentions evolved social norms (Li et al. 2024) and the AI Economist tax policy (Zheng et al. 2020). No discussion of externalities, tragedy of the commons, multi-principal settings, or welfare/Kantian acceptance. Acceptance rules are not a design dimension; Sec. 5.1 lists "how to evaluate" (performance, novelty, safety, robustness). Limitations: "Safety and governance are not operationalized." Cites no paper comparing acceptance rules across agents in a shared environment. Read in full (first 100k of 191k). — [Survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10299)
- **Internal vs. External** (arXiv:2605.09128; Niranjani, Kumar, Tan; 9 May 2026). Compares agent deliberation (majority-vote amendments, App. C.2) vs. centralized OpenEvolve evolution (MAP-Elites, 30 iterations, 3 islands, population 10) of a single society-wide constitution; fitness is the collective Stability Score 0.5P + 0.3V − 0.2C. Results (10 seeds): gridworld .257/.319/.458 (control/deliberation/evolution), public goods .336/.376/.472, trading n.s.; at multiplier m=0.75 evolution falls to worst (.477 vs. .536). No deliberation run proposed punishment. Does not compare individual vs. collective criteria, no price on externality, matched compute not reported in seen text; no Kantian/universalization/Pigouvian terms. This is the nearest of the named set (mixed-motive public goods + comparison of rule-adoption mechanisms) but the factor is who designs the group rule, not per-agent acceptance gates. Read in full (first 100k of 108k). — [Internal vs External](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09128)
- **Economy of Minds** (arXiv:2606.02859; Qi et al.; v1 1 Jun 2026, v2 13 Oct 2026). LLM agents bid in auctions for the right to act; winners pay the previous actor; rent each episode; bankrupt agents removed and replaced by mutating rich agents or amending weak ones (Sec. 2.2–2.3, App. A Alg. 1). No pre-entry acceptance check (novice bid guarantees first win). Prices act as credit assignment along an action chain, not externality correction. Ablations remove auctions/exploration/exploitation, vary rent and reward scale, and compare bid schemes; no welfare vs. private comparison. No Kantian/welfare/externality/Pigouvian terms. Read in full (first 100k of 157k). Overlap: it uses prices among co-evolving agents, so it is the nearest relative to the project's "price" arm, but in a cooperative credit-assignment role. — [Economy of Minds](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02859)
- **Darwin Gödel Machine** (arXiv:2505.22954). Archive acceptance: only agents that compile and retain codebase-editing ability are added; parent selection roughly proportional to performance and inversely to number of children (details in App. C.2, not seen). Ablations "DGM w/o self-improve" and "DGM w/o open-ended exploration" (Sec. 4.3); App. A.3 parent-selection ablation not seen; budget matching not confirmed in the read portion. Single objective, independent benchmark evaluation; no multi-agent externalities or welfare. Read in full (first 100k of 234k). — [DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954)

### Inferences
- None of the named papers overlaps on the project's experimental factor. "Internal vs External" and "Evolving Interpretable Constitutions" (same group) are the closest named-adjacent works because they use mixed-motive environments and welfare-style fitness, and "Economy of Minds" is closest on prices; these should be cited as contrasts.
- DGM/Hyperagents ablations show that "acceptance rule as factor" exists for single lineages (archive vs. hill-climb), which the project should acknowledge as the single-agent precursor.

### Gaps
- Late appendices of long papers (DGM App. A.3/C.2; Hyperagents App. E.5; second halves of Misevolve, MASS, survey) were beyond the fetched window; a targeted check of those appendices would confirm no multi-agent gate comparisons there (unlikely given main-text design).

## Q3. Related lines: coevolutionary fitness, EGT on selection rules, opponent shaping, PBT interference, prosocial reward shaping at matched budget, and other 2026 work

### Takeaway
Adjacent lines vary objectives or learning rules, not accept/reject gates over proposed self-modifications, and few report matched-budget comparisons. Newly found 2026 works relevant to positioning: SAGE, PACE, the Skill–Policy trap, Red Queen Gödel Machine, the Safety in Self-Evolving Agents survey (which notes shared-state propagation across agents but no other-regarding gates), and the Kantian Equilibrium workshop paper.

### Cited Findings
- Opponent shaping: M-FOS treats shaping as a meta-game and critiques LOLA as myopic, asymmetric, and requiring white-box higher-order gradients; COALA-PG (arXiv:2410.18636) reports outperforming M-FOS and LOLA, with fair cooperation on CleanUp-lite. These compare learning rules for selfish agents; no matched-budget comparison was found in snippets; abstract/snippet only. — [M-FOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01447); [COALA-PG overview](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2410.18636v2)
- Prosocial reward shaping in MARL: McKee et al. (2020) introduced SVO reward sharing; Madhushani et al. (2023) heterogeneous SVO yields diverse policies; SocialJax (2025) benchmarks common vs. individual rewards, SVO and reward exchange in sequential social dilemmas; 2026 AFP (altruistic+fairness preference) reward sharing. These vary the reward each agent optimizes, the analogue of the project's private vs. welfare gates, but as training objectives in RL, not acceptance gates in propose–evaluate–accept loops. Snippet only. — [SocialJax](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14576); [McKee et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02325.pdf); [Heterogeneous SVO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.00768); [AFP](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.04710)
- Red Queen Gödel Machine (arXiv:2606.26294, Iacob et al., Cambridge, 24 Jun 2026): co-evolves agents and their evaluators; scoring criterion fixed within an epoch and updated at epoch boundaries; reports 1.35–1.72x fewer tokens than HGM-H on coding and less biased reviewers. It changes the evaluator, not a multi-agent other-regarding gate. Snippet only. — [arXiv:2606.26294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26294); [Cambridge news](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/misc/news/2026/07-21-red-queen/)
- Safety in Self-Evolving Agents survey (arXiv:2610.00093): notes unsafe influence can "propagate across carriers or agents" and teams "propagate plans and findings through shared state" (Sec. 1, 2.1); gating is defined per state object; Takeaway 2.1 "Utility Does Not Authorize Promotion"; no externality, welfare or Kantian criteria. Cites MAPLE-Guard, ACIArena, EvoMap for multi-agent propagation risks. Read (body queried via HTML; PDF fetch failed). — [Survey HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00093v1)
- Interference in shared self-modification state: "Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners?" (arXiv:2610.25858) reports global edits to a shared prompt/workspace causing cross-task interference, but within one agent; CoMAS (arXiv:2510.08529, ICLR 2026) self-evolves via inter-agent interaction without external reward, without interference analysis. Snippet only. — [arXiv:2610.25858](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25858); [CoMAS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08529)
- Cooperative coevolution: Agogino–Tumer difference evaluations (see Q1) are the strongest classical precursor for comparing private/global/marginal evaluation of evolving agents. — [GECCO 2005](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gecco2005/docs/p591.pdf)

### Inferences
- The project's design (per-agent accept/reject gate as factor, κ-mixture Kantian gate, Pigouvian price comparator, matched budget) appears to bridge three literatures that have not been combined: statistical acceptance gates for self-evolving agents (SAGE/PACE), Kantian/Homo moralis economics (Alger–Weibull, Roemer), and multiagent fitness shaping/prosocial reward (Agogino–Tumer, SVO). The novelty claim is defensible if phrased as "no prior work found that compares acceptance gates of multiple self-improving agents sharing an environment with externalities", while citing those lines as nearest precursors.

### Gaps
- Population-based training interference (e.g., PBT exploit/explore rules in shared environments) was not searched separately; no source gathered.
- Multi-agent evolutionary game theory on imitation/selection rules (e.g., comparisons of imitation dynamics vs. best response) was not searched beyond Alger–Weibull and Roemer; no source gathered.
- No matched-budget comparison of prosocial vs. selfish reward shaping was confirmed from full text.
